## I. Welcome to Paradise (Terms and Conditions Apply)

**Castaway Island** is a small, interactive, animated 3D diorama about a long-haired, magnificently bearded castaway and his tireless attempts to make himself comfortable in extremely limited circumstances.

It is part miniature stage, part living screensaver, and part illustrated chronicle of human ingenuity encountering a coconut. The sea rolls in. The palm sways. The gentleman devises a plan. The island expresses its opinion.

The world performs short comic scenes approximately every **30 seconds**, mixing idle island life with increasingly elaborate physical comedy. There is no score to chase and no need to supervise every minute. You may watch an entire absurd little life unfold, or simply leave the island to get on with it.

**The island's official motto:** *There is always tomorrow.*

The castaway has long hair, a beard with a life of its own, expressive eyes, large gestures, and an admirable ability to mistake minor progress for major triumph. He is neither especially lucky nor willing to admit defeat.

The island is intentionally small: each tree, wave, footprint, gesture, and misplaced object plays a part in the joke.

**A word about sound:** The island is quiet until you enable audio. Browsers require a visitor's interaction before allowing it. Once invited, procedural ambience and comic effects can accompany the waves and mishaps.

**A word about patience:** The animation continues autonomously. Missing a scene is no cause for alarm; the island is not in a hurry.

## IV. An Illustrated Guide to Things That Might Happen

Every episode is a tiny wordless performance: an idea, an earnest attempt, the suggestion of success, a reversal, a silent reaction, and a return to ordinary island business.

A few entries from the castaway's unofficial field journal:

### The Portable Shade

The sun is merciless. Our hero engineers a personal canopy from a palm leaf. A breeze rotates it behind his head. He follows the shade in a solemn circle, then sits underneath the perfectly serviceable tree.

*Lesson learned: nature has already invented several of your inventions.*

### Diplomatic Relations

A crab approaches. The castaway puts on his finest leaf glove and extends a ceremonial hand. The crab accepts the glove rather than the greeting and departs with it. Diplomatic relations remain cordial, if poorly understood.

*Lesson learned: never offer a crab anything you need back.*

### The Perfect Timepiece

Pebbles are aligned. A driftwood marker is calibrated. The sun's shadow is measured with scholarly precision. A palm frond shifts and produces an entirely different reading. The scientist retires for a nap.

*Lesson learned: time is relative, especially beneath a tree.*

### Return to Sender

A distress message is folded, bottled, corked, and entrusted to the open sea. The current returns it to the quay. A crab kindly completes the delivery. The castaway acknowledges the efficient postal service.

*Lesson learned: your correspondence has been received.*

### The Magnificent Pier

After careful inspection, the castaway congratulates himself on the quay. The tide withdraws beyond its reach. He considers the new distance to the ocean, then posts a small sign: **OPEN TOMORROW**.

*Lesson learned: infrastructure should occasionally consult the sea.*

These are original Castaway vignettes, not reproductions of scenes from another game. Additional routines, incidents, and longer story chapters can join the island as production evolves. The comedy depends on clear silhouettes, an unhurried setup, a sharply readable reversal, and a dignified reaction to the undignified.

## V. How Island Time Works

A typical episode lasts **30 seconds**. The first moments establish calm; the middle develops a questionable solution; the final moments make room for consequences and a return to rest. The timing can feel almost musical: a long, hopeful pause is often funnier than another explosion.

The story director keeps track of which original scenes have played, discourages immediate repetition, and can unlock situations when their prerequisites have occurred. For example, the crab must make an acquaintance before becoming the island's postal worker. Where available, progress is saved locally in the browser.

There is no promise of a rigid episode order. The island prefers a certain continuity, but reserves the right to surprise its guest.

### The Castaway's Rules of Conduct

1. Every problem deserves an overengineered solution.
2. Every solution deserves a moment of pride.
3. Every moment of pride is vulnerable to the tide.
4. No misfortune is complete until the beard has stopped moving.
5. Tomorrow will almost certainly be different. The ocean makes no guarantees.

## VI. Natural History and Sound

The sea is not simply a painted backdrop. The miniature world uses 3D geometry, lighting, shadows, water motion, and staged props to frame the comedy. Expressive body acting, grounded contacts, secondary movement, and small environmental reactions help the world feel alive even when nobody is doing anything important.

When enabled, sound is synthesized procedurally rather than serving as an endless prerecorded soundtrack. Surf, wind, and lightly punctuated effects are intended to leave room for silence. A well-timed sigh can communicate more than a full orchestra.

The intended presentation is gentle and readable: the island remains a peaceful screensaver between bouts of magnificent incompetence.

## VII. Frequently Asked Questions from Concerned Relatives

**Can he escape?** He is working on it. He has also become rather attached to the view.

**Is there a winning condition?** Not presently. Watching is the point; the castaway provides the ambition.

**Why doesn't he build a better boat?** Please address engineering proposals to the gentleman with the leaf hat.

**Is the crab friendly?** The crab is professional.

**Can I turn off the sound?** Sound begins disabled; use the audio control to enable it when desired.

**Does the story end?** Short scenes recur and vary. Larger story arcs are an area of ongoing development; do not mistake a planned storyline for a fully implemented ending.

**Is this the old screensaver?** No. Castaway Island is a new work, inspired by the tradition of classic wordless screensaver comedy, particularly the spirit of small absurd adventures. Its characters, models, gags, code, and staging are original. It is not affiliated with the creators or rights holders of *Johnny Castaway*.

## VIII. Notes for the Island's Maintenance Department

*The following pages are intended for people who insist on fixing things that are already funny.*

### Technology and prerequisites

The project is a browser-based Three.js diorama developed with **Vite+**. The repository currently declares **Three.js 0.186.1** and **vite-plus 1.3.0**. Install the project dependencies using the package manager appropriate to your environment and lockfile before running commands.

| Command | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| `vp dev` | Run the development server |
| `vp build` | Build the production site |
| `vp preview` | Preview the production build |
| `vp check` | Run the configured project checks |
| `vp test` | Run the configured tests |

The site is published using **GitHub Pages**. The public entry point is [xtreemze.github.io/castaway](https://xtreemze.github.io/castaway/). For local development, use the development server rather than opening the source HTML as a file; the application loads JavaScript modules and bundled dependencies.

### The machinery behind the misfortune

- `story-catalog.mjs`: approved original episode definitions, props, prerequisites, consequences, six causal phases, and accessible scene summaries.
- `story-director.mjs`: deterministic scene selection, recently played history, progression flags, and the 30-second story clock.
- `comedy-acting.mjs` and related animation modules: per-beat performances and motion.
- `src/main.js`: the deliberately small DOM shell—input capture, accessibility labels, local persistence and user-activated audio.
- `src/scene-worker.js` and `src/scene-runtime.js`: OffscreenCanvas WebGL worker and its reusable fallback runtime; world construction, characters, story timing, scene animation and GPU submissions live here.
- `src/audio/ocean-noise-processor.js`: ocean-noise synthesis on the AudioWorklet rendering thread; the host retains AudioContext setup, cues, and routing.
- `.github/workflows/pages.yml`: GitHub Pages workflow.

### Rendering without occupying the visitor's main thread

When supported, the island transfers its canvas to a dedicated Web Worker via `transferControlToOffscreen()`. The worker owns the entire Three.js scene and animation loop; the main thread only forwards pointer/resize/control messages and applies concise story labels and progress updates. Pointer movement is batched and scene-to-DOM messages are deduplicated. The ocean surface is displaced by the vertex shader rather than rewriting 3,721 vertices and recomputing their normals in JavaScript every frame.

Audio is separate from the graphics worker: after an explicit **Enable sound** interaction, an AudioWorklet synthesizes surf noise on the browser's audio rendering thread. Its short comic cue scheduling remains with the AudioContext host. If AudioWorklet is unavailable, a precomputed looping buffer is used. If offscreen WebGL workers are unavailable or cannot initialize, the same runtime runs on the main thread; this avoids excluding older browsers and devices.

Workers cannot use the DOM, localStorage, or audio activation APIs. The host is responsible for these browser-owned operations. Because workers share the same CPU/GPU budget as the rest of the page, offloading improves responsiveness but does not guarantee more frames per second. Rendering remains resolution-capped at a device-pixel ratio of 2.

Each new episode should have an original premise, a readable setup, anticipation, escalation or misunderstanding, an unmistakable payoff, aftermath, and recovery. Give it a unique ID, six meaningful phases, accurate prerequisites and effects, accessible summary, and a complete return to a stable island state. Episodes in the current registry use a 30-second duration.

Please distinguish **implemented features** from **story pitches**. A wonderful joke written in an issue is not yet a wonderful joke on the screen.

### Contributing to the expedition

The source and issue tracker live at [xtreemze/castaway](https://github.com/xtreemze/castaway). [The narrative and animation bible](https://github.com/xtreemze/castaway/issues/1) records the comedy language, historical research, and candidate ideas. Historical reference material should remain clearly separated from original production assets and scripts.

When proposing a scene, explain *why it is funny before explaining how to animate it*. Prefer a precise visual reversal over dialogue; leave space for stillness, reaction, and the island's continuing existence. Run `vp check`, `vp test`, and `vp build` before proposing a merge where your development environment supports them.

### A final notice

Castaway Island is a work in progress. Some planned narrative chapters, interactions, accessibility refinements, and presentation improvements may not yet be present in the live build. The manual describes the existing experience and its intended creative direction without guaranteeing unfinished features.
